# Troop Class Cultural Equipment Summary

This file rewrites the normalized class summaries into a human-readable reference focused on troop role, named equipment, and the cultures that rely on each class most heavily.

## Light Infantry

Description: Levy footmen and tribal retainers used to screen the line, hold rough ground, and provide cheap mass.

Main cultures: Dacian, Germanic, Britonic, and Caledonian warbands rely on this class most heavily, with lighter Caucasian, Parthian, Judean, and Bosporan variants also present.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Dacian: Dacian short or medium spears, Dacian oval shields, pileus caps, light Dacian armor, and Celtic boots.
- Britonic and Caledonian: Gallic spears or clubs, Celtic round or large shields, Britton Coolus helmets, Celtic light armor, and Celtic boots.
- Germanic: Germanic war spears or shortened spears, Germanic round shields, Germanic caps, linen tunics or light Germanic armor, and Celtic boots.
- Caucasian: Armenian or Caucasian spears, Scythian round shields, Sarmatian or Phrygian caps, Armenian tunics, and Sarmatian shoes.
- Parthian and Judean: Eastern spears, Parthian daggers or old gladii, light Eastern shields, Persian headcloths or Roman auxiliary helmets, Persian or Judean tunics, and Eastern shoes.

Common pattern: A spear, a light shield, cloth or leather clothing, and simple headgear define the class.

Notable outliers: Some Egyptian and Judean variants look closer to Roman-trained auxiliaries, with pila, hastae, or heavier shields, reflecting Roman influence in their recruitment and training.

## Medium Infantry

Description: The professional backbone of organized armies, able to hold formation and fight sustained melee.

Main cultures: Roman forces dominate this class by a large margin. Britonic, Caledonian, Germanic, Judean, Arabian, Nubian, and Syrian troops appear as secondary traditions.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Roman: Roman gladii or spathae, scuta or cetratus shields, Imperial Gallic helmets, and legionary hamata, squamata, or segmentata.
- Britonic and Caledonian: Gallic spears, Celtic swords, Celtic shields, Britton helmets, Celtic mail shirts, and Celtic boots.
- Germanic: War spears, Germanic large shields, Germanic helmets, Germanic mail armor, and Celtic boots.
- Eastern auxiliaries: Eastern spears, sicas, or swords, round or oval shields, Roman or regional helmets, scale or mail corselets, and regional shoes.

Common pattern: A shielded spear-and-sword infantryman in mail or scale, clearly more disciplined and better protected than levy troops.

Notable outliers: Roman auxiliary units often mix Roman helmets with distinctly Eastern or local armor traditions, reflecting the diverse influences in their recruitment and training.

## Heavy Infantry

Description: Elite armored foot soldiers meant to anchor the battle line, storm strong points, and win close combat by staying power.

Main cultures: Roman, Germanic, Caucasian, Parthian, Dacian, and Bosporan armies all rely heavily on heavy infantry.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Roman: Roman gladii, legionary scuta, crested Imperial Gallic helmets, segmentata, hamata, or squamata, manica arm guards, and armored caligae.
- Germanic: Saxes, Germanic spathae, Cheruski swords, Germanic shields, Germanic helmets, heavy or noble Germanic armor, and noble tunics.
- Britonic and Caledonian: Celtic swords or spathae, Celtic long shields or Pict square shields, Britton noble helmets, heavy Celtic armor, and Celtic boots.
- Caucasian: Armenian or Caucasian swords, maces, Eastern heavy shields, Armenian heavy helmets, Armenian scale or mail, and Eastern shoes.
- Parthian: Eastern swords or military hammers, Eastern heavy shields, Parthian heavy helmets, Parthian scale or mail, and Eastern shoes.
- Dacian and Bosporan: Dacian ring swords, noble swords, spears, Dacian or Bosporan shields, Dacian heavy helmets or Bosporan spangenhelms, medium-to-heavy armor, and Celtic boots or Sarmatian shoes.

Common pattern: Heavy infantry usually carries a sword or axe sidearm, a large shield, a proper helmet, and full mail, scale, or segmented armor.

Notable outliers: Germanic berserker-style variants can appear nearly unarmored, relying instead on skins and body paint rather than heavy protection.

## Light Cavalry

Description: Fast mounted scouts and raiders used for reconnaissance, pursuit, and flank harassment.

Main cultures: Sarmatian forces are the clear and almost exclusive users of this class.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Sarmatian: Light or medium lances, Sarmatian ring swords, Sarmatian caps or light helmets, kaftans or light Sarmatian armor, Sarmatian shoes, and steppe horses.

Common pattern: A light lance, a ring sword, light armor, and a fast steppe horse.

Notable outliers: None. This class is strongly tied to steppe cavalry traditions.

## Medium Cavalry

Description: Balanced horsemen capable of charging, skirmish pursuit, and line support without the full weight of cataphracts.

Main cultures: Roman, Caucasian, and Caledonian traditions are most visible here, with Germanic, Sarmatian, and Syrian examples also present.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Roman: Hastae, Roman spathae, cetratus cavalry shields, Imperial Gallic cavalry helmets, cavalry hamata or squamata, graves, and normal horses.
- Caucasian: Eastern spears, Kartli axes, Scythian cavalry shields, Armenian heavy helmets, Armenian scale or mail, and Parthian horses.
- Caledonian and Germanic: Gallic or Germanic spears, Celtic or Germanic shields, Britton or Germanic helmets, medium armor, and normal horses.
- Sarmatian: Cavalry spears, war axes, Scythian cavalry shields, Bosporan or Sarmatian helmets, light Scythian or Sarmatian armor, and steppe horses.
- Syrian: Syrian or Eastern spears, round shields, pilos-style or Eastern helmets, Syrian scale or Arabian tunics, and Arabian horses.

Common pattern: A spear-first cavalryman with a shield, a decent helmet, medium armor, and a regionally appropriate horse breed.

Notable outliers: Roman and Syrian units in this class often look like cultural hybrids rather than purely native cavalry traditions.

## Heavy Cavalry

Description: Noble or elite shock cavalry built around the decisive lance charge.

Main cultures: Sarmatian, Syrian, Roman, and Dacian armies are the main users, with smaller Caledonian, Germanic, and Saka entries.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Sarmatian: Heavy lances, Sarmatian ring swords, pointed or heavy Sarmatian helmets, Sarmatian scale armor, leather gloves, and steppe or half-cataphract horses.
- Syrian: Lances, Eastern swords or Palmyran gladii, round shields, heavy Parthian-style cavalry helmets, Syrian scale, and half-cataphract Parthian horses.
- Roman: Hastae, Roman spathae, Roman or Praetorian cavalry shields, decurio or Praetorian cavalry helmets, musculata or heavy hamata, armored caligae, and normal horses.
- Dacian and Saka: Lances, Dacian or Alan sidearms, heavy helmets, heavy armor, and steppe or cataphract-capable horses.

Common pattern: A heavy lance, a secondary sword, a strong helmet, and scale or mail armor built for the charge.

Notable outliers: Roman elite cavalry sometimes uses prestige mounts or officer cuirasses rather than the more typical steppe or Eastern heavy-cavalry equipment set.

## Super Heavy Cavalry

Description: Cataphracts and comparable aristocratic cavalry, where both rider and mount are heavily armored.

Main cultures: Caucasian, Parthian, and Bosporan forces rely on this class most clearly.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Caucasian: Caucasian long swords, Armenian axes, heavy spears, Scythian cavalry shields, Sarmatian heavy helmets or Bosporan pointed helmets, heavy scale or mail, and cataphract steppe horses.
- Parthian: Heavy lances, cataphract axes, maces, cataphract helms or sallets, cataphract eastern armor, mail mittens, cataphract boots, and cataphract Parthian horses.
- Bosporan: Sarmatian ring swords, heavy lances, Bosporan pointed helmets, Bosporan or Sarmatian scale armor, and cataphract steppe horses.
- Syrian: Palmyran gladii, old spears, round shields, mak-style helmets or pilos helmets, Palmyran lamellar armor, and Arabian horses.

Common pattern: Super heavy cavalry is defined by full-body armor, a cataphract lance, enclosed or very heavy helmets, and horses built to bear armor.

Notable outliers: Syrian cataphract-style troops appear lighter in their mounts, often keeping Arabian horses instead of full cataphract breeds.

## Light Skirmishers

Description: Loose-order missile troops for harassment, disruption, and softening the enemy before heavier troops close in.

Main cultures: Germanic, Caucasian, Parthian, and Judean forces make the strongest use of this class, with smaller Britonic, Caledonian, Roman, and Greek examples.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Germanic: Clubs or short spears, javelins or slings, Germanic shields, Germanic caps, very light armor or simple tunics, and Celtic boots.
- Caucasian: Spears, javelins, slings, mixed round shields, Sarmatian or Phrygian caps, Armenian tunics or Persian sheepskin coats, and Sarmatian shoes.
- Parthian: War axes, pickaxes, daggers, javelins or slings, light round shields, Phrygian caps or Persian headcloths, Persian tunics, and Eastern shoes.
- Judean: Old spears, javelins, mixed round or hide shields, headcloths or Eastern helmets, Judean tunics, and Eastern shoes.
- Roman and Greek: Gladii or xiphoi, slings with lead bullets, hide-covered round shields, straw hats, and minimal armor.

Common pattern: A light melee backup weapon, one cheap missile system, a small shield, and almost no armor.

Notable outliers: Mediterranean slingers look very different from northern javelin troops, especially because of their straw hats and lead sling ammunition.

## Medium Skirmishers

Description: Better-equipped missile infantry who can keep throwing or slinging after the battle line begins to tighten.

Main cultures: Caucasian, Parthian, Judean, and Germanic armies rely on this class most often.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Caucasian: Spears or axes, javelins or slings, mixed round shields, Sarmatian or Phrygian caps, Armenian tunics, sheepskin layers, and Sarmatian shoes.
- Parthian: Hammers, daggers, or axes, javelins or slings, mixed or Scythian shields, Phrygian caps, Persian headcloths, Parthian or Persian tunics, and Eastern shoes.
- Judean: Spears or gladii, javelins or slings, mixed round shields, headcloths or Roman auxiliary helmets, Judean tunics, graves, and sometimes mail.
- Germanic: Clubs, axes, or Cheruski swords, javelins or slings, Germanic shields, Germanic caps or helmets, and light Germanic armor.
- Britonic, Caledonian, and Lombard: Gallic spears or clubs, throwing spears, Celtic or Germanic shields, wolf-skin or Britton headgear, and tribal leather, fur, or tunic armor.

Common pattern: Compared with light skirmishers, this class gains sturdier shields, better sidearms, and a clear move toward helmets and armor.

Notable outliers: Lombard skirmishers stand out for their wolf-skin headdresses and rawhide or pelt-heavy look.

## Light Archers

Description: Cheap or lightly trained foot archers used for harassment and ranged support rather than decisive missile duels.

Main cultures: Parthian, Germanic, and Dacian forces rely on this class most visibly, with smaller Roman, Judean, Bosporan, Syrian, Britonic, and Caledonian entries.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Parthian: Eastern spears, Persian bows, standard or barbed arrows, Persian headcloths, tunics, and Eastern shoes.
- Germanic: Saxes, maces, or Cheruski swords, German shortbows or long bows, arrows, Germanic caps, and light Germanic armor.
- Dacian: Spears or ring swords, nomad bows, arrows, pileus caps, and light Dacian armor.
- Judean and Bosporan: Old gladii or Sarmatian ring swords, short bows or Khergit bows, arrows, Roman auxiliary helmets or Sarmatian caps, and light armor.
- Syrian: Spears, Arabian bows, Syrian barbed arrows, turbans or Phrygian caps, Arabian tunics or robes, and Eastern shoes.

Common pattern: A simple bow, a sidearm, minimal armor, and enough ammunition for harassment rather than sustained heavy exchange.

Notable outliers: Roman troops in this band often lean toward sling traditions rather than classic archery.

## Medium Archers

Description: Regular army archers with enough armor and training to keep firing under pressure.

Main cultures: Roman auxiliaries dominate this class. Dacian, Germanic, Judean, Bosporan, Nubian, Syrian, and Egyptian troops appear as smaller traditions.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Roman: Roman gladii, short bows, long bows, Persian bows, or nomad bows depending on unit origin, arrows or bodkin and barbed ammunition, Roman auxiliary helmets, graves, and hamata or squamata.
- Dacian and Germanic: Spears, ring swords, maces, or axes, nomad bows or German bows, arrows, pileus caps or Germanic caps, and light-to-medium armor.
- Judean and Bosporan: Old gladii or Sarmatian swords, nomad or Khergit bows, arrows, Roman auxiliary helmets or Bosporan spangenhelms, and Judean or Bosporan armor.
- Nubian and Egyptian: Nubian short swords, Nubian war bows or short bows, bodkin arrows, Numidian helmets or wigs, kilts or light armor, and gauntlets in elite Nubian cases.
- Syrian: Spears, Arabian bows, Syrian barbed arrows, pilos or Eastern helmets, Syrian scale, Arabian tunics, and robes.

Common pattern: Better helmets, better ammunition, and noticeably more armor separate medium archers from levy bowmen.

Notable outliers: Roman archer units vary strongly by province, so their weapons and armor often reflect the culture that recruited them rather than a single Roman template.

## Heavy Archers

Description: Elite foot missile troops whose armor and ammunition let them stand closer to the battle line and hit harder.

Main cultures: Dacian and Parthian forces are the strongest users, with smaller Britonic, Caledonian, Germanic, Judean, Bosporan, Arabian, Syrian, Greek, and Roman specialist examples.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Dacian: Spears or ring swords, nomad or Khergit bows, arrows or barbed arrows, pileus caps or heavy helmets, and medium Dacian armor.
- Parthian: Eastern swords, Khergit bows, Khergit or barbed arrows, Parthian pointed helmets or cataphract sallets, heavy Parthian scale, and Eastern shoes.
- Roman: Roman gladii, ballistae with bolts for specialist artillery troops, Imperial Gallic helmets, and subarmalis or light armor.
- Germanic and Britonic: Heavy axes or clubs, long bows or war bows, arrows, Germanic caps or Britton helmets, and medium tribal armor.
- Judean, Syrian, Arabian, and Greek: Gladii or spears, nomad, Arabian, or war bows, bodkin or barbed arrows, Roman auxiliary or Eastern helmets, and mail, scale, or padded armor.

Common pattern: Strong bows, armor-piercing ammunition, and better armor than ordinary archers.

Notable outliers: Roman ballistarii belong here by battlefield role, but they are artillerymen rather than true bowmen.

## Light Horse Archers

Description: Fast mounted archers built for hit-and-run fire, reconnaissance, and exhausting slower enemies.

Main cultures: Sarmatian forces field this class almost exclusively.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Sarmatian: Sarmatian bows, Sarmatian arrows, light or medium lances, Sarmatian caps or Bosporan helmets, kaftans or light armor, Sarmatian shoes, and steppe horses.

Common pattern: Bow first, lance second, very light armor, and a fast steppe mount.

Notable outliers: Veteran versions can begin to resemble medium horse archers because their armor improves more quickly than their weapons.

## Medium Horse Archers

Description: Professional mounted archers who combine reliable shooting with enough armor and melee ability to survive a closer fight.

Main cultures: Caucasian and Sarmatian armies rely on this class most, with Parthian, Syrian, Roman, and Saka examples also present.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Caucasian: Eastern swords, strong bows, arrows or Khergit arrows, Phrygian caps or Parthian cavalry helmets, Armenian tunics or scale armor, and steppe horses.
- Sarmatian: Light or heavy lances, Khergit bows, Sarmatian arrows, Alan or Sarmatian helmets, kaftans or medium Alan armor, and steppe horses.
- Parthian: Eastern swords, Persian bows, barbed or Khergit arrows, Parthian cavalry helmets, Parthian tunics, and Parthian horses.
- Roman: Roman spathae, hastae, nomad bows, bodkin arrows, Armenian heavy helmets, squamata or capes, and Parthian horses.
- Syrian and Saka: Spears or ring swords, Arabian or Khergit bows, regional arrows, heavy steppe or Arabian-style headgear, and Arabian or steppe horses.

Common pattern: A mounted archer with a real cavalry sidearm, better armor, and a culturally specific horse breed.

Notable outliers: Roman eastern auxiliaries in this class often look more Armenian or Parthian than conventionally Roman.

## Heavy Horse Archers

Description: Elite mounted archers who combine steppe-style archery with the armor and impact power of serious cavalry.

Main cultures: Sarmatian forces rely on this class most heavily, followed by Caucasian armies. Parthian and Syrian forces field smaller heavy horse archer traditions.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Sarmatian and Scythian: Heavy lances or cavalry spears, Sarmatian or Khergit bows, Sarmatian arrows, Alan helmets, Sarmatian heavy helmets, kaftans, Sarmatian mail or scale, and steppe or half-cataphract Parthian horses.
- Caucasian: Armenian axes, swords, or spears, strong bows, Khergit or barbed arrows, Armenian heavy helmets, Armenian mail or scale, heavy Parthian-style armor, and steppe horses.
- Parthian: Eastern swords, Persian bows, barbed arrows, Parthian cavalry helmets, Parthian tunics, and Parthian horses.
- Syrian: Spears, Arabian bows, Syrian barbed arrows, heavy Parthian-style cavalry helmets, Syrian scale, and Arabian horses.

Common pattern: Heavy horse archers always carry a powerful bow, better armor than medium horse archers, and a lance or spear that lets them act as shock cavalry when needed.

Notable outliers: Scythian cataphract-style variants push this class close to true cataphracts, with heavily armored horses and very heavy body armor.

## Light Skirmisher Cavalry

Description: Fast desert javelin cavalry used for harassment, scouting, and operating in terrain unsuitable for heavier horsemen.

Main cultures: Berber and Garamantian forces are the only clear users of this class.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Berber: Numidian spears, Berber javelins, African or mixed round shields, Sarranid felt hats or turbans, light Numidian armor, caligae, and Numidian horses.
- Garamantian: Numidian spears, Berber javelins, African shields, headcloths or feather bands, Garamantian armor, caligae, and Numidian horses.

Common pattern: A light spear, a bundle of javelins, desert headgear, and a fast Numidian horse.

Notable outliers: None. The class is tightly tied to North African and desert warfare traditions.

## Medium Skirmisher Cavalry

Description: Mounted harriers who can throw javelins, scout ahead, and still take part in short melee actions.

Main cultures: Parthian forces are the clearest users, with Britonic and Judean troops also relying on the class. Roman eastern auxiliaries appear as a smaller branch.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Britonic: Gallic spears, Celtic swords, javelins, Celtic round or large shields, Britton helmets with crests or plumes, Celtic armor, and normal horses.
- Parthian: Eastern spears, Eastern swords, javelins, Scythian cavalry shields, Parthian cavalry helmets or Phrygian caps, mail or scale, and Parthian horses.
- Judean: Old spears, javelins, old round shields, Roman auxiliary or townguard helmets, Judean mail, graves, and Parthian horses.
- Roman: Roman spathae, javelins, cetratus cavalry shields, Roman auxiliary helmets, auxilia squamata or Eastern-style mail shirts, and Arabian horses.

Common pattern: A javelin cavalryman with a shield, a spear or sword for backup, and medium armor.

Notable outliers: Roman and Judean variants again show very strong Eastern influence in both mounts and armor.

## Heavy Skirmisher Cavalry

Description: Armored javelin cavalry that sits between pure skirmishers and true shock cavalry.

Main cultures: No single culture dominates; Britonic, Judean, Arabian, Berber, and Garamantian troops all contribute a small but distinct version of the class.

Typical equipment by culture:
- Britonic: Gallic spears, Celtic swords, javelins, Celtic shields, noble Britton helmets, heavier Celtic armor, and normal horses.
- Judean: Old spears, javelins, round shields, Roman auxiliary or townguard helmets, Judean mail or scale, centurion-grade graves, and Parthian horses.
- Arabian: Arabian swords or old spears, throwing spears, mixed round shields, Eastern helmets or padded desert hats, Sarranid mail shirts or cavalry robes, and Arabian horses or camels.
- Berber and Garamantian: Numidian spears, Berber javelins, African shields, Eastern helmets or turbans, Numidian or Garamantian armor, centurion-grade graves, and Numidian horses.

Common pattern: The class keeps javelins as its defining weapon but upgrades to stronger armor, better helmets, and more prestigious mounts.

Notable outliers: Arabian heavy skirmisher cavalry uniquely includes camel-mounted troops, making it the most distinctive cultural variant in the entire class.
