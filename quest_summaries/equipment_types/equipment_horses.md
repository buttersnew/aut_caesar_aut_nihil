### MOUNTS (WARHORSES)
*(The beasts bred and trained for the chaos of ancient battlefields.)*

*   **Tribal Pony / Light Horse**
    *   **Speed:** 60 ft.
    *   **Carrying Capacity:** 225 lbs.
    *   *Details:* Small, shaggy, and incredibly hardy. Used by Germanic tribesmen, British charioteers, and the legendary Numidian light cavalry of North Africa. They are not meant to charge into infantry lines; they are meant to skirmish, harass, and outrun the enemy.
*   **Gallic / Auxiliary Cavalry Horse**
    *   **Speed:** 60 ft.
    *   **Carrying Capacity:** 400 lbs.
    *   *Details:* The standard warhorse of the Roman *Alae* (cavalry wings) and wealthy Celtic nobles. Carefully bred in Gaul and Spain, these are disciplined animals trained to ignore the noise of battle, push into enemy lines, and bite or kick in melee.
*   **Nisaean Charger (Eastern Heavy Horse)**
    *   **Speed:** 50 ft.
    *   **Carrying Capacity:** 540 lbs.
    *   *Details:* A massive, highly prized breed from the plains of Parthia (modern Iran) and the Sarmatian steppes. They are specifically bred to carry the immense weight of *Cataphract* armor (scale armor for both rider and horse) while maintaining a terrifying charge.

### TACK & RIDING GEAR
*(How a warrior stays on their mount without stirrups.)*

*   **Bareback Pad / Riding Blanket**
    *   **Cost:** Cheap
    *   *Details:* A simple woolen blanket or animal skin strapped to the horse’s back. Favored by the poorest tribal riders and the elite, ultra-mobile Numidian cavalry who control their horses solely with a neck rope and knee pressure.
    *   **Mechanic:** Wielding a two-handed weapon (like a Longbow) or a Heavy shield from a Bareback Pad imposes Disadvantage on Animal Handling checks to stay mounted when taking damage.
*   **The Four-Horn Saddle (Roman / Celtic Saddle)**
    *   **Cost:** Expensive
    *   *Details:* The technological marvel of ancient cavalry. Because stirrups don't exist, Celtic engineers invented a leather-over-wood saddle with four rigid "horns" that tightly grip the rider's thighs, locking them onto the horse. The Romans adopted it universally. It allows a rider to slash with a sword, thrust a spear, or block with a shield without falling off.
    *   **Mechanic:** Grants Advantage on any Animal Handling or Strength saving throw made to resist being disarmed of your mount, pushed out of the saddle, or knocked prone while riding.
*   **Calcaria (Spurs)**
    *   **Cost:** Moderate
    *   *Details:* Bronze or iron spikes strapped to the rider's heels. Since ancient cavalrymen had both hands full (a shield in one hand, a spear or sword in the other), they couldn't hold the reins during combat. Spurs allowed them to steer the horse with their feet.
    *   **Mechanic:** If riding with a Four-Horn Saddle and Spurs, you can control your mount perfectly without using your hands, allowing you to fight normally with a shield and weapon.

### BARDING (HORSE ARMOR)
*(Armor crafted specifically to protect mounts. It is heavy, incredibly expensive, and mostly found in the East or among elite commanders.)*

*   **Lorica Squamata Half Barding (Cataphract Scale Armor)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 8
    *   **Mechanic:** Disadvantage on the horse's Stealth; reduces horse's speed by 5 ft.
    *   *Details:* Covers the front half of the horse. Massive, overlapping bronze or iron scales sewn onto a leather backing that covers the horse from neck to knee. This transforms the horse into a living tank. Historically used almost exclusively by Parthian, Armenian, and Sarmatian shock cavalry (*Cataphracts*) charging with the long *Kontos* spear.

*   **Lorica Squamata Barding (Cataphract Scale Armor)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 16
    *   **Mechanic:** Disadvantage on the horse's Stealth; reduces horse's speed by 10 ft.
    *   *Details:* Covers the entire horse. Massive, overlapping iron scales sewn onto a leather backing that covers the horse from neck to knee. This transforms the horse into a living tank. Historically used almost exclusively by Parthian, Armenian, and Sarmatian shock cavalry (*Cataphracts*) charging with the long *Kontos* spear.

### VEHICLES
*(Wheeled warfare. By the 1st century AD, chariots are completely obsolete in mainland Europe and Rome—used only for racing and parades—but they are still actively used in the British Isles.)*

*   **British War Chariot (Esseda)**
    *   **Capacity:** 1 Driver, 1 Warrior
    *   **Draft:** Pulled by 2 Light Tribal Ponies
    *   *Details:* Unlike the heavy, lumbering chariots of the ancient Middle East, the Celtic chariots of Britain (famously used by Boudicca against the Romans) are light, incredibly fast, and feature a unique suspension system made of woven rope.
    *   **Combat Tactics:** They are not used to crash into enemy lines. Instead, the driver races parallel to the enemy. The warrior runs out onto the chariot pole (between the two horses) to hurl javelins (*Light Throwing Spears*), before the chariot retreats. If forced into melee, the warrior dismounts to fight on foot, using the chariot as a mobile retreat point.
*   **Carrus (Roman Supply Wagon)**
    *   **Capacity:** 2-4 tons of cargo
    *   **Draft:** Pulled by 2 to 4 Mules or Oxen
    *   **Details:** The heavy, slow-moving logistical backbone of the Roman Legion. Two-wheeled or four-wheeled, groaning under the weight of tents, artillery pieces (ballistae), grain, and blacksmithing tools. Ideal targets for ambushes in the dark forests of Germania.
