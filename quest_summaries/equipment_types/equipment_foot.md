### BAREFOOT & WRAPS
*(The poorest levies, skirmishers, and fanatics fight with little to no foot protection, relying on thick calluses.)*

*   **Barefoot**
    *   *Details:* Slingers, skirmishers, and many tribal warriors fought barefoot to maximize their grip on the earth and move silently through the forests.
    *   **Mechanic:** You have Advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks in natural environments, but you suffer 1d4 piercing damage if you move through difficult terrain like brambles, jagged rocks, or Roman caltrops (*tribuli*).
*   **Fasciae (Leg Wraps / Puttees)**
    *   *Details:* Strips of wool or linen wrapped tightly from the ankle to the knee. Worn by poor tribesmen, hunters, and increasingly by Roman legionaries stationed in the freezing forests of Germania or the bogs of Britannia.
    *   **Mechanic:** Grants Advantage on Constitution saving throws against Extreme Cold and prevents movement penalties from thick underbrush.

### CIVILIAN & TRIBAL FOOTWEAR
*(Soft leather shoes designed for daily life, hunting, or surviving brutal winters.)*

*   **Carbatinae / Turnshoes**
    *   *Details:* The universal shoe of the ancient peasant and tribal warrior. Made from a single piece of cowhide wrapped around the foot and stitched at the top. They are completely flat with no rigid sole.
    *   **Mechanic:** Standard footwear. No specific bonuses, but they allow for relatively quiet movement compared to heavy military boots.
*   **Germanic Winter Boots (Fur-Lined)**
    *   *Details:* Thick leather boots reaching the mid-calf, lined with bear or wolf fur, and tied with leather thongs. Absolutely essential for surviving a campaign across the Rhine during the winter.
    *   **Mechanic:** You ignore difficult terrain caused by deep snow or ice.

### MILITARY MARCHING TACK
*(Engineered for war, discipline, and grinding marches.)*

*   **Caligae (Roman Hobnailed Sandals)**
    *   *Details:* The legendary marching boot of the Roman Legionary. It is a heavy, open-work leather boot with a thick sole pounded full of iron hobnails. They are incredibly well-ventilated to prevent trench foot, and the iron studs allow legionaries to dig their feet into the mud during a shield-wall push.
    *   **Mechanic (The Iron Grip):** You have Advantage on Strength or Dexterity saving throws against being knocked Prone while standing on dirt, mud, or grass.
    *   **Mechanic (The Stone Slip):** Hobnails on smooth stone or marble act like ice skates (historically documented as a fatal flaw during urban sieges, i.e. Flavius Josephus mentiones a Roman centurio running on the Temple square in Jerusalem during the siege of 70 AD and slipping after which he cant get up quickly enough and is thus killed by the Judaean rebels). You have Disadvantage on Dexterity (Acrobatics) checks on paved roads or inside stone temples, and your footsteps "clack" loudly, imposing Disadvantage on Stealth.
*   **Calcei (Closed Military Boots)**
    *   *Details:* Closed-toe leather boots without the heavy hobnails of the *caligae*. Worn by Roman officers (Centurions, Tribunes) and wealthy civilian magistrates.
    *   **Mechanic:** A status symbol. Wearing red *calcei* marks you as a Patrician or high-ranking officer, granting Advantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks when dealing with Roman citizens or soldiers.

### EQUESTRIAN GEAR
*(Equipment specifically designed for surviving long hours on horseback without stirrups.)*

*   **Braccae (Celtic / Germanic Trousers)**
    *   *Details:* Wool or linen trousers, often woven in bright, plaid-like patterns (the ancestor of tartan). Romans viewed them as barbaric, but Roman cavalry and auxiliaries rapidly adopted them to prevent their legs from being flayed raw by their saddles.
    *   **Mechanic:** Wearing *Braccae* allows you to ride a horse for a full day without suffering exhaustion from saddle-chafing. However, wearing them in the city of Rome or before a conservative Senator imposes Disadvantage on Charisma checks, as you are dressed like a "savage."
*   **Parthian / Scythian Riding Boots**
    *   *Details:* Soft, thigh-high leather boots with completely flexible soles. Because stirrups don't exist, Eastern horse-archers need to be able to feel the flanks of their horse with their feet to steer them during combat.
    *   **Mechanic:** Grants a +1 bonus to Wisdom (Animal Handling) checks made to control a mount in combat, but their lack of a rigid sole gives you Disadvantage on Constitution saving throws made to endure Forced Marches on foot.

### ARMORED LEGWEAR
*(Metal protection for the lower body, historically used because large shields did not cover the shins.)*

*   **Ocrea (Bronze / Iron Greaves)**
    *   *Details:* Form-fitting metal shin guards, tied on with leather straps over padding.
    *   **Special (The Asymmetrical Defense):** In the 1st Century, Greek and Eastern troops wear two greaves. However, Roman Legionaries and Gladiators (like the *Secutor*) typically wear **only one greave on their left leg**, because the left leg is the one thrust forward from behind their massive *Scutum* (shield).
    *   **Mechanic:** Wearing at least one greave grants you a +1 AC specifically against Attacks of Opportunity (as you step away from enemies) and reduces damage from hidden ground traps (like caltrops or low tripwires) by half.
*   **Articulated Leg Guard (The Gladiator's Manica for the Leg)**
    *   *Details:* Interlocking bands of iron or hardened leather that cover the entire leg from hip to ankle. Used almost exclusively by highly specialized gladiators (like the *Cruppellarius*) or heavily armored Parthian Cataphracts.
    *   **Mechanic:** Grants +1 AC to your overall armor class, but the sheer weight and awkwardness reduce your walking speed by 5 feet.
