### UNARMORED / TRIBAL DEFENSE
*(Many Celtic and Germanic warriors fought in simple tunics or entirely naked, relying on shields, mobility, and sheer psychological terror.)*

*   **Sky-Clad (Naked / War Paint)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 10 + Dex modifier
    *   *Details:* Celtic *Gaesatae* and certain Germanic berserkers notoriously fought completely naked, wearing only a neck-torc and blue *woad* paint to show their absolute fearlessness and trust in their gods.
    *   **Special (Psychological Warfare):** If you enter combat wearing no armor and no clothing, you gain Advantage on Charisma (Intimidation) checks against civilized/Roman troops who are unsettled by your fanaticism.

*   **Tunic**
    *   **Armor Class:** 10 + Dex modifier
    *   *Details:* A simple woolen or linen tunic, often belted at the waist. The most basic form of clothing in the ancient world. It is worn by everyone from the poorest tribal levies to the wealthiest Roman senators (who would just wear a finer, more expensive tunic). It offers no real protection, but it is comfortable and allows for maximum mobility.

*   **Kaftan**
    *   **Armor Class:** 10 + Dex modifier
    *   *Details:* A long, loose tunic that reaches the knees or ankles, often belted at the waist. It is a common garment among the Eastern tribes and nomadic peoples. It offers no real protection, but it is comfortable and allows for maximum mobility.

*   **Decorated Tunic**
    *   **Armor Class:** 10 + Dex modifier
    *   *Details:* A tunic adorned with decorative embroidery, dyed with expensive colors, or trimmed with fur. It is a status symbol of wealth and prestige among tribal chieftains and nobles from all cultures. It offers no real protection, but it is comfortable and allows for maximum mobility.
    *   **Special (Status Symbol):** Wearing a decorated tunic grants Advantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks when interacting with other tribal leaders or Roman aristocrats, as it signals your high status and wealth.

### LIGHT ARMOR
*(Favored by skirmishers, hunters, and light infantry. It offers basic protection without hindering mobility or stealth.)*

*   **Subarmalis / Thoracomachus (Padded Garment)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 11 + Dex modifier
    *   *Details:* A thick, heavily quilted tunic made of wool or linen. Romans wore this *under* their metal armor to absorb blunt force trauma and prevent chafing, but poorer auxiliaries and tribal levies wore it as their only layer of defense. It often features *Pteruges* (the iconic leather or linen strips hanging over the shoulders and waist).
*   **Linothorax / Spolas (Layered Linen Armor)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 12 + Dex modifier
    *   *Details:* This armor is technically an anachronism, as it came out of fashion already at the late republic era, when the remaining diadochi states began adopting Roman styles. Originally a Greek invention, this armor is made of multiple layers of linen glued or quilted tightly together, sometimes reinforced with a thin layer of leather. It is incredibly stiff, lightweight, and surprisingly effective at stopping arrows.

### MEDIUM ARMOR
*(Offers a balance of protection and mobility. Worn by Roman cavalry, standard officers, and tribal warriors of moderate wealth.)*

*   **Cardiophylax (Bronze Pectoral)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 13 + Dex modifier (max 2)
    *   *Details:* A simple, square or circular bronze plate strapped over the chest and back, worn over a tunic. It protects the vital organs but leaves the stomach and arms exposed. A very ancient Italian design, still used by poorer auxiliary troops or Iberian/Illyrian tribal warriors.
*   **Lorica Squamata (Scale Armor)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 14 + Dex modifier (max 2)
    *   **Properties:** Disadvantage on Stealth
    *   *Details:* Hundreds of small bronze or iron scales sewn onto a fabric or leather backing, overlapping like fish scales. Heavily used by Sarmatians, but also spread around the world by the Romans. It is favored by Roman Centurions, standard-bearers, and cavalry because it is flexible and easily repaired by individual soldiers.
    *   *Vulnerability:* Historically, scale armor is weak to an upward thrust. If an enemy attacks you from a prone position, or attacks from a lower elevation, they gain a +1 to their attack roll.
*   **Lorica Hamata - Short (Chainmail Shirt)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 15 + Dex modifier (max 2)
    *   *Details:* **Chainmail was invented by the Celts**, and it remains the prized possession of Celtic and Germanic chieftains. A short shirt of interlocking iron rings that reaches the waist. It is heavy, but incredibly flexible and breathes well in the European heat.

### HEAVY ARMOR
*(Worn by the elite: Roman Legionaries, ultra-wealthy tribal warlords, and heavily armored gladiators. It requires high strength to bear the weight.)*

*   **Lorica Hamata - Long (Legionary Chainmail)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 16
    *   **Properties:** Strength 13 Required, Disadvantage on Stealth
    *   *Details:* The absolute standard-issue armor of the Roman Republic and still heavily used by 1st-century Legionaries and Auxiliaries. It reaches down to the mid-thigh and features thick shoulder-doubling (a cape-like flap of extra chainmail across the shoulders) to protect against the devastating downward slashing of Celtic longswords.
*   **Alanic Lamellar (Medium Lamellar)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 16
    *   **Properties:** Strength 13 Required, Disadvantage on Stealth
    *   *Details:* The Alans were a nomadic Iranian people who lived in the steppes north of the Black Sea. They were famous for their lamellar armor, which consisted of small, overlapping iron plates sewn onto a leather backing.
*   **Lorica Segmentata (Articulated Plate Bands)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 17
    *   **Properties:** Strength 15 Required, Disadvantage on Stealth
    *   *Details:* The technological pinnacle of 1st-century warfare, and the iconic "Roman Legionary" armor. Made of broad iron hoops bound together by internal leather straps and brass hinges. It offers unparalleled protection against slashing weapons, but does not cover the arms or lower legs.
    *   **Special (High Maintenance):** Segmentata rusts incredibly fast and the leather straps snap easily. If you roll a natural 1 on a Dexterity saving throw, a strap snaps, reducing the AC to 16 until you spend 10 minutes repairing it.
*   **Palmyrene Lamellar (Heavy Lamellar)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 17
    *   **Properties:** Strength 15 Required, Disadvantage on Stealth
    *   *Historical Context:* Palmyra was a wealthy desert metropolis between the Roman and Parthian empires. Stelae show elite warriors wearing full suits of iron lamellar that covered the torso, often combined with laminated arm and leg guards.
    *   **Special (Rigid Construction):** Lamellar is exceptionally good at stopping blunt force. You gain a +1 bonus to AC against weapons that deal **Bludgeoning** damage.


### SUPER HEAVY ARMOR
*(The absolute pinnacle of ancient armor technology. It is so heavy and cumbersome that it is only used by the most elite shock troops, and it requires a high strength score to even wear.)*
*   **Cruppellarius Armor (Fully Enclosed Iron)**
    *   **Armor Class:** 18
    *   **Properties:** Strength 15 Required, Disadvantage on Stealth, Reduces Speed by 10 ft.
    *   *Details:* In 21 AD, a Gallic revolt featured *Cruppellarii*—gladiators encased completely in continuous iron bands (essentially primitive full plate). They were practically invulnerable to swords and javelins, but so heavy and clumsy that Roman legionaries had to use pickaxes and poles to knock them over and crack them open like tin cans.
*   **Parthian Cataphract Armor**
    *   **Armor Class:** 18
    *   **Properties:** Disadvantage on Stealth, Reduces Speed by 10 ft.
    *   *Details:* The Parthian shock cavalry (*Cataphracts*) were the most heavily armored troops of the ancient world. It would consist of a combination of mail and plate or scale armor, covering the rider from head to toe. The horse would also be armored with scale barding. This made them nearly invulnerable to slashing attacks, but they were still vulnerable to piercing weapons like spears and arrows.
    *   **Special (The Charge):** When a Cataphract makes a charge attack with a Kontos spear, they can choose to gain Advantage on the attack roll, but if they miss, they are vulnerable to opportunity attacks until the end of their next turn as they struggle to recover from the failed charge.
    *   **Special (No boots):** Boots and greaves are already included in the armor, so the Cataphract cannot wear additional leg armor.
