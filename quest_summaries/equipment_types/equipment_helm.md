### ORNAMENTATION, REINFORCEMENTS & THE RULE OF FURS/FEATHERS
Before looking at the helmets themselves, it is crucial to understand that ancient warriors heavily customized their headgear. People frequently wore animal furs or feathers in addition to a helmet, or entirely on their own, and many helms were further reinforced after manufacture.

*   **Feathers Only:** It is typical for Libyan and Numidian skirmishers to fight with a pair of ostrich feathers woven directly into their hair, wearing no other headgear.
*   **Animal Pelts (Signiferi & Berserkers):** Roman standard-bearers (*Signiferi*) wore wolf or bear pelts pulled entirely over their helmets. Germanic warriors frequently did the same, believing they channeled the animal's spirit.
*   **The Germanic "Frankenstein" Combo:** A typical sight in the deep forests of Germania is a tribal champion wearing a looted Roman helmet (usually with the original cheek and neck guards stripped away), topped with a bristling crest of eagle feathers, and draped in a heavy fox fur that hangs down over the shoulders.
*   **Helmet Reinforcements:** Most helmets in the setting, especially Celtic, Germanic, Dacian, Thracian, Sarmatian, and Eastern types, can be upgraded with riveted scale or mail neck guards and with additional cheek guards. The Roman helmet family is the major exception, because Roman designs already come with purpose-built cheek-pieces and neck-guards as part of the base construction.
*   **Mechanic:** Wearing predatory furs or prominent tribal feathers grants a +1 bonus to Charisma (Intimidation) checks.

### CIVILIAN & SOFT HEADGEAR
*(Offers no real armor protection, but provides crucial protection against the elements.)*

*   **Generic Cap (Pileus / Pilleus)**
    *   **Protection:** None
    *   **Coverage:** Weather protection only
    *   *Details:* A simple, brimless felt or wool cap worn by sailors, workers, and tradesmen. In Rome, it is the absolute symbol of a freed slave (*libertus*): a slave would be given this cap upon manumission and wear it proudly as a symbol of freedom. It is also popular among the poorest tribes across Europe.
*   **The Petasos**
    *   **Protection:** None
    *   **Coverage:** Sun and rain protection
    *   *Details:* A broad-brimmed hat made of woven straw, felt, or leather. Originally Greek, but universally worn by travelers and messengers across the Mediterranean to keep the glaring sun and rain out of their eyes.
    *   **Mechanic:** Grants Advantage on Constitution saving throws against extreme heat or sun dazzle.
*   **Phrygian Cap**
    *   **Protection:** None
    *   **Coverage:** Warmth and light weather protection
    *   *Details:* A soft, conical cap with the top pulled forward and pointing down. Worn heavily by civilians and light troops in Anatolia, Thrace, Dacia, and Parthia. In Roman art, this cap is the universal shorthand for "someone from the East."

### LIGHT & OPEN HELMETS
*(Favored by cavalry, archers, and Hellenic holdovers. Designed for maximum visibility and hearing, at the cost of facial protection.)*

*   **Greek Helmets (Boeotian)**
    *   **Protection:** Light
    *   **Coverage:** Skull and brow, with excellent hearing and visibility
    *   *Details:* By the 1st century, Greek helmets are already old-fashioned, but some Hellenophile Lugians and antiquarian nobles may still commission them. The Boeotian helmet is essentially a folded bronze hat resembling a metal Petasos.
*   **Conical Helm / Early Spangenhelm**
    *   **Protection:** Light
    *   **Coverage:** Skull, with optional added neck or cheek reinforcement
    *   *Details:* A tall, pointed helmet. Sometimes forged from a single piece of bronze, but increasingly made by riveting vertical iron plates together, or even by binding shaped horn plates over a frame. Highly popular among Sarmatian horsemen and wealthier Eastern Germanic chieftains, but also spread across Parthia and the Caucasus.
*   **Phrygian Helmet (Metal)**
    *   **Protection:** Light
    *   **Coverage:** Skull and partial cheeks
    *   *Details:* A bronze or iron helmet mimicking the shape of the Phrygian cap, complete with a forward-leaning peak at the crown. Used heavily by Dacian infantry, Thracian mercenaries, and Eastern kingdoms. It often features large cheek-pieces, and the peak helps deflect downward blows.

### MEDIUM & HEAVY HELMETS
*(Designed for the brutal crush of frontline melee. They protect the neck and face, but restrict peripheral vision and hearing.)*

*   **Roman Helm Variants (Coolus & Montefortino)**
    *   **Protection:** Medium
    *   **Coverage:** Skull, small neck guard, and basic cheek flaps
    *   *Details:* The older style of Roman helmets, slowly being phased out but still incredibly common in the mid-1st century. Made of spun bronze, they are shaped like simple domes with a modest neck-guard and basic cheek protection.
*   **Roman Helm Variants (Imperial Gallic & Italic)**
    *   **Protection:** Heavy
    *   **Coverage:** Skull, ears, deep cheeks, and a massive neck guard
    *   *Details:* The iconic, state-of-the-art iron Roman helmet. It features deep hinging cheek-pieces, cut-outs for the ears, a massive flaring neck-guard to protect against slashing swords, and a thick reinforced brow-ridge.
    *   **Special (Anti-Slashing):** The heavy brow-guard was specifically engineered to stop the terrifying downward strike of the Dacian *Falx* and Celtic longsword. If you are hit by a critical strike dealing slashing damage while wearing this, you can roll a 1d4 and reduce the damage by that amount.
*   **Crested Celtic Helmet (Agen / Port Styles)**
    *   **Protection:** Medium
    *   **Coverage:** Skull first; cheeks and neck often added later as custom fittings
    *   *Details:* Celtic nobility took immense pride in their helmets. These are deeply bowl-shaped iron or bronze helms, famously topped with elaborate, intimidating crests. Some feature iron horns, massive bronze birds with hinged wings that flap as the warrior runs, or the Celtic "Solar Wheel."
    *   **Mechanic:** The extreme ornamentation makes you a highly visible leader on the battlefield, increasing the range of your inspiring commands or auras by 10 feet.
*   **"Zombified" Roman Helm (The Trophy Helm)**
    *   **Protection:** Medium
    *   **Coverage:** Variable, depending on what survives and what has been rebuilt
    *   *Details:* A Roman Imperial helmet looted during battle or a raid and then kept in service for years by a Germanic or Celtic warrior. Broken cheek-pieces are replaced by stitched leather flaps, rust is scraped away, the neck-guard is battered flat, and the whole thing is adorned with boar tusks, bronze wire, and tribal runes. It is a terrifying status symbol of a veteran Roman-killer.

### SPECIALIZED / HEAVY CAVALRY HELMETS

*   **Parthian Cataphract Helm**
    *   **Protection:** Heavy
    *   **Coverage:** Full head, face, neck, and throat
    *   *Details:* The absolute heaviest headgear of the era, worn strictly by the elite shock cavalry of the Parthian Empire and Sarmatian steppes. It consists of a tall conical iron helmet attached to a complete chainmail aventail, or sometimes a polished metal face-mask, that covers the entire face, neck, and throat, leaving only two tiny slits for the eyes.
    *   **Mechanic:** You are immune to critical hits from ranged weapon attacks, and you gain resistance to inhaled poisons or dust. However, because you are looking through tiny eye-slits and your ears are covered in heavy iron mail, you suffer Disadvantage on all Wisdom (Perception) checks relying on sight and hearing.
